Hitscan is a shot that lands the instant you click. No travel time, no bullet drop, no leading the target. That's it. That's the whole thing. The arguments start afterward.

## A Quick History of Hitscan

The hitscan origin trick is older than the word. Wolfenstein 3D (1992) couldn't afford to simulate flying bullets on period hardware. The gun never spawned a projectile at all: it drew an invisible line from the muzzle, checked what it crossed, and dealt damage immediately, like an [aimbot](/slang/aimbot-gaming/) with paperwork. DOOM (1993) ran the same math into legend. "Hit" plus "scan" entered the vocabulary once designers had to explain why some weapons needed to be led and others didn't.

Quake and Counter-Strike turned it into the default dialect of shooters. Arena players called the railgun the purest aim test ever shipped. CS rifles made "point at head, remove head" a religion, and I've whiffed point-blank shots that still visit me at night, so instant doesn't mean free. Meanwhile games chasing ballistic flavor, Battlefield, PUBG, went the other way and made you calculate drop like an artillerist.

## Why Hitscan Stuck Around

Because it's honest math and cheap servers. Instant feedback feels fair in both directions: you clicked the head, or you didn't. Balance teams love it too, since damage drop-off is a slider instead of a physics exam.

| | Hitscan | Projectile |
|---|---|---|
| Travel time | none | speed, drop, lead |
| Feels like | a laser pointer | lobbing rocks, lovingly |
| Usual suspects | CoD rifles, CS2 AK | PUBG snipers, rockets |

Projectile diehards call it flat. The faithful call it pure. Nobody clips a [no-scope](/slang/no-scope-gaming/) and praises bullet physics, but both sides keep shipping games, so the stalemate continues. My ranked teammates still yell "it's point-and-click, how did you miss" with zero irony. Fair question. Working on it. Why do gamers say hitscan with that much conviction? Thirty years of forum reps will do that.

## FAQ

**Hitscan vs projectile: what's the real split?**

Travel time. Your shot resolves the frame you click, while a projectile physically flies, drops and can be dodged. The hitscan vs projectile argument sounds theological because it is: one side worships reflex, the other worships prediction.

**What does hitscan mean for my aim?**

Crosshair placement over everything. There's no flight time to blame, so your [headshot](/slang/headshot-gaming/) rate is exactly as honest as your crosshair discipline. Keep it at head level, pre-aim corners, and suddenly "how did he kill me" has a boring answer.

**Which big games are mostly hitscan?**

Call of Duty, CS2 and Valorant are the famous ones; Overwatch mixes both by hero. Apex and PUBG lean projectile. In those lobbies the word lands as a straight compliment.

**How would you explain it to a non-gamer?**

A laser pointer versus a thrown ball. The laser dot arrives when you click; the ball arrives when physics says so. One tests your reflexes, the other tests your forecasting. Shooters have spent thirty years arguing about which is purer.
