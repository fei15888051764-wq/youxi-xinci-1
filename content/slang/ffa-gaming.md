The first time I heard FFA, I thought it meant something official. Like a government agency. Turns out it's just chaos with better branding.

FFA stands for "Free For All." Everyone versus everyone. No teams. No [squad](/slang/squad-gaming/)mates to blame. Just you, your aim, and nine other people who all decided you're the problem. It's the most honest game mode ever invented. Also the most infuriating.

## A Quick History of FFA

The mode predates online gaming. Local multiplayer on GoldenEye 007? That was the mode. Four players, one TV, no alliances. The term itself became standardized in the early 2000s when [FPS](/slang/fps-gaming/) games like Quake III Arena and Unreal Tournament needed a label for "everyone shoots everyone."

Before it, shooters had deathmatch. But deathmatch was vague — some games used it for team modes, some for solo. It made it explicit. No teams. Every kill counts for you and only you. The clarity mattered. In a [PvP](/slang/pvp-gaming/) environment, knowing who your enemies are is kind of important. In FFA, the answer is "everyone."

Halo popularized it on console. Call of Duty made it a playlist staple. By 2010, every major shooter had this mode. Now? Even battle royales flirt with it. Solos mode is basically the same with extra steps and a shrinking circle.

## How You'll Hear It Used


> **friend:** "Wanna run some games?"
> **you:** "FFA or teams?"
> **friend:** "FFA. I don't trust you on my team anyway."
> **you:** "Fair."

It's casual. It's quick. And it's perfect for warming up because you're fighting actual humans without the pressure of letting down teammates.

Or in a lobby after a match:

> **random:** "GG, that was close."
> **you:** "Yeah, FFA is pure anarchy."
> **random:** "For real. I got third-partied like four times."

The phrase works because it's descriptive. You say "FFA" and everyone instantly knows the social contract: there is no social contract. Shoot first, ask never.

## Why Gamers Can't Quit FFA

Because it's ego in game mode form.

In team modes, you can blame your support. You can blame your IGL. You can blame lag, hitreg, whatever. In this mode, there's nobody to blame but yourself. You died? Your fault. You won? Your skill. It's refreshing in a weird way. Brutal, but fair.

FFA is also where a lot of players learn to aim. No team distractions. No objective to worry about. Just pure gunplay. Pros warm up there. Streamers grind camos there. Casual players blow off steam there. It serves every audience because it asks nothing of you except "be better than the other nine people."

And honestly? Sometimes you just want to shoot your friends without feeling bad about it. The mode is socially acceptable betrayal. That's valuable.

## FFA vs TDM vs Squad

Not every multiplayer mode works the same. Here's the quick breakdown:

| Mode | Teams | Goal | Vibe |
|------|-------|------|------|
| **FFA** | None | Most kills wins | Chaotic, selfish, honest |
| **TDM** | Two | First to score limit | Teamwork, coordination, blame-sharing |
| **Squad** | 3-4 | Be last team standing | Communication, strategy, friendship tests |

It's where you go when you want to test yourself. TDM is where you go when you want to hang out. Squad is where you go when you want to find out which of your friends can't follow a callout.

## FAQ

**Is FFA the same as battle royale?**

Not exactly. BRs have one winner (or one squad), looting phases, and a shrinking map. It's just a deathmatch with no teams. You respawn. You keep playing. There's no plane, no circle, no building mechanics. It's simpler. Some would say purer. Others would say boring. Both are valid.

**When should I play FFA instead of ranked?**

When you want to warm up without losing LP. When you're mad and need to shoot things without ruining someone's promo series. When your friends aren't online and you still want to play multiplayer. It's the default "I just want to play" mode.

**Do people still play FFA?**

Constantly. Every Call of Duty has it. Every Halo has it. Valorant has it in the range (sort of). Overwatch has deathmatch lobbies. It's not trendy — it's infrastructure. Like [ping](/slang/ping-gaming/) or [lag](/slang/lag-gaming/). It just exists.
