You're twelve minutes into a ranked match when the enemy entry fragger deletes you through a smoke, mid-strafe, before he even renders on your screen. Not luck. Not hacks. A prefire.

## Where Prefire Came From

Where did prefire come from? It's short for preliminary fire, and the idea predates the label: if you know the angle, shoot the angle. Early Counter-Strike regulars turned it into muscle memory on maps everyone had memorized, where heads appear in the same five places forever. As CS rolled into CS:GO and then CS2, and tactical shooters like Valorant copied the homework, the term moved from team comms into everyday vocabulary. These days there are whole workshop maps and training modes (Yprac, Refrag) dedicated to drilling it. That's how you know a skill went core: people pay money to practice it.

## Why Prefire Stuck Around

Because information wins rounds. If your intel says a head lives on that corner, starting the shot early flips a losing [peek](/slang/peek-gaming/) into an execution. The cost is real: you announce yourself and burn ammo. So the rule I play by stays simple. Prefire the position, not the player. A spot with a name your whole team recognizes earns early bullets. Random walls? That's just fear with a trigger. Why do gamers say prefire like it's a personality trait? Because map knowledge is one.

### Pro Play Corner

Watch any Major demo with x-ray on and count the kills that start before the victim's crosshair arrives. Tier 1 AWPers became walking [one-tap](/slang/one-tap-gaming/) compilations off prefired pixels, timed from footsteps, utility sounds and hundreds of demo hours. Casters don't even say "preliminary fire" anymore. The short version is simply expected at that level.

## Prefire in the Wild

Half the time it's coaching, the other half it's cope. Real prefire examples from comms and all-chat.

Round 24, 11-12, you're first through the smoke:

> **Iggy:** prefire corner, he's always there
> **Bo:** always?
> **Iggy:** *shoots* always
> **Bo:** okay he was there

Demo review with the team coach:

> **Coach:** why'd you spray that wall on B
> **Vik:** common off-angle, awpers hold it
> **Coach:** and this time?
> **Vik:** they held A instead

All-chat, right after a filthy round:

> "nice wallhack prefire bro"
> "it's called map knowledge, try the tutorial"
> "the tutorial doesn't teach my exact spot"
> "your spot is called ramp. everyone knows ramp"

Funny thing: the accusation is a compliment. Nobody calls hacks on a whiffed spray. Clip the ones that land and you're basically a [wallbang](/slang/wallbang-gaming/) highlight channel.

## FAQ

**Prefire vs spray-and-pray: same thing with confidence?**

No, and your VOD review will prove it. Spray-and-pray is noise: you shoot because you're nervous. A prefire is a claim about the map: this exact angle, this exact timing, because the info says so. One has a hit rate. The other has a mood.

**When should I go for the prefire?**

When the spot is famous and your utility or intel says someone's home. Common corners, default plants, the angle every awper holds. Skip it when you're broke on ammo or when silence matters more. What does prefire mean for your econ? About fifty bullets a half, if you're honest.

**How would you explain it to someone new to shooters?**

Like swinging at a piñata you know is behind the door, except the door is a smoke and the piñata has an AWP. You shoot where the enemy will be, not where they are. When it works, it looks like magic. When it whiffs, it looks like your rank.
