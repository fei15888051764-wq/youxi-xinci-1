catJAM. One cat. Infinite groove.

If a stream's music kicks in and chat doesn't instantly flood with a bobbing white cat, either the channel's tiny or everyone's asleep. I'm a Mario Kart casual and even I installed BetterTTV for this little guy.

## The Origin of catJAM

Where did catJAM come from? The cat's name is Minette. On April 15, 2020, TikTok user @micklagi posted a video of her bobbing along to "I Wanna Dance" by Jonas Blue, and the internet did what it does. On July 24, MadLittleCat uploaded it to BetterTTV as an emote. Four days later, an r/xqcow petition got xQc to add it to his channel, and that was the starting gun. By mid-August over 12,600 channels had it enabled. Sad footnote: Minette got rehomed in fall 2020 (the owner's kid turned out to be allergic), but the vibe lives on.

## The catJAM Effect

Most emotes react to a moment. This one creates the moment. Drop any beat and it just works, zero context needed. Streamers treat it as a free [hype](/slang/hype-gaming/) button, and unlike [KEKW](/slang/kekw-gaming/), the joke's never at anyone's expense. My honest observation after years of lurking: a catJAM chain is the most coordinated thing Twitch viewers ever pull off. Nobody conducts. Nobody counts in. Somehow everyone lands on the same beat anyway. Why do gamers say catJAM is chat's favorite ritual? Because nobody has to organize it.

## Common Questions

**catJAM vs PogChamp: what makes it different from other hype emotes?**

It's rhythm-based, and that's the whole trick. [PogChamp](/slang/pogchamp-gaming/) reacts to something cool that already happened. This emote needs nothing at all: one person drops it during a song and the whole chat syncs up. Participation, not commentary.

**Why do I only see plain text instead of the cat?**

You're on vanilla Twitch. It's a BetterTTV emote, so you need the browser extension (or a channel's own uploaded version) to see the actual cat. The plain mobile app just shows raw text, which honestly reads like a weird typo.

**Did catJAM peak in 2020, or is it still around?**

Still around. It skipped the "cringe to use" phase that kills most emotes, mostly because it's tied to music instead of some streamer's face. New viewers pick it up within a week. The cat outlived the meta. So what does catJAM mean in 2026? The same synchronized little party it always was.
