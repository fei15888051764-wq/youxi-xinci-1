It's 47 minutes into the game and your carry just facechecked the enemy jungle alone. No wards, no map, no fear. You know how this ends. So does chat.

BibleThump is the [emote](/slang/emote-gaming/) for exactly that moment: the crying face of Isaac from The Binding of Isaac, spammed whenever something on stream goes genuinely (or mockingly) sad.

## BibleThump: The Origin Story

Where did BibleThump come from? The face comes straight out of The Binding of Isaac, Edmund McMillen's 2011 indie game about a very unhappy kid. Isaac's weapon is literally his own tears, which makes it weirdly on-brand. Twitch picked the sobbing sprite for its official global roster early on, and it became the default way to say "that hurt to watch." No extensions needed, no unlocks. Just a crying child, available to everyone.

## Why BibleThump Stuck Around

Every emote family has a sadness slot, and this one owns the "real tears" tier. It reads sincere where other sad faces read theatrical, so it survives every shift in chat fashion while trendier faces come and go. After a decade of bot lane and Twitch chat, my take is simple: people keep it because sometimes you're not being ironic. Sometimes the run actually hurt. Why do gamers say BibleThump instead of plain crying emojis? Ten years of muscle memory.

| Emote | Sadness level | When chat reaches for it |
|---|---|---|
| BibleThump | Actually crying | Real tragedy: dead runs, goodbye streams, pet cams |
| [Sadge](/slang/sadge-gaming/) | Quietly bummed | Disappointment you accept with a sigh |
| [PepeHands](/slang/pepehands-gaming/) | Dramatic sobbing | Mock grief, hamming it up for the bit |

**Meme moment:** any speedrun that dies at the final boss. Chat doesn't wait for the reaction cam. The wall of crying faces starts before the death animation even finishes.

## FAQ

**BibleThump vs Sadge: which one do I pick?**

Short answer: match the weight. BibleThump is for stuff that genuinely stings, like a lost run or a duo partner quitting the game. Sadge is for milder disappointment, the sigh-and-move-on kind. Spam the wrong tier and chat will judge you. Ask "what does BibleThump mean" and the short version is: chat's crying button.

**Can I use it outside Twitch?**

Yep. It shows up in Discord servers, Twitter replies, and group chats as a reaction image. Anyone who watches streams will read it instantly, no explanation needed.

**What game is the crying kid from?**

The Binding of Isaac, a 2011 indie roguelike where a naked child fights monsters in a basement using his tears. Cheerful stuff. It's honestly one of the lighter things in that game.
