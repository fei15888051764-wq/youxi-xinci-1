Every new player learns this the hard way. Ngl, I spent my first week getting wrecked before I figured out attachments were a thing. You jump into a match with the default gun, get melted by someone with a custom [meta](/slang/meta-gaming/) build, and realize: oh, I can change my stuff. That's your kit. Your identity. The reason you die or don't.

In gaming, this is everything you're bringing into the fight. Weapons, attachments, perks, abilities, [skins](/slang/skin-gaming/)—the whole package. Military types have used this word for decades. Gamers stole it around 2007 when Call of Duty 4 let you build a class from scratch.

## How It Became a Thing

Before this concept, everyone started equal. GoldenEye gave you a character and a gun. Halo gave you an AR and a dream. Then Call of Duty 4: Modern Warfare dropped Create-a-Class, and everything changed. Suddenly, you could pick your gun, your attachments, your perks, your equipment. It wasn't just a gun anymore. It was a full kit.

The concept exploded. Warzone has Drops. Apex Legends has loot-based kits. Valorant has fixed-agent kits with buy-phase variation. Every shooter now expects you to care about your setup. And YouTube definitely cares—search any gun and you'll find fifty videos promising the "BEST SETUP."

## In Real Conversations

**Pre-game lobby:**

> **Friend:** "What's your setup for this map?"
> **You:** "Sniper, Overkill, and a dream."
> **Friend:** "So you're camping."
> **You:** "I'm holding angles. There's a difference."

The pre-game conversation is ritual. It's theorycrafting. It's hope. You spend ten minutes building the perfect class, die in thirty seconds, and immediately blame the gun. (Then you copy the guy who killed you. We all do it.)

## Why Everyone Says It

This turned shooters into RPGs. The gun is no longer just a gun—it's part of a build. Your kit is a statement. Rush setup. Camp setup. Meme setup. The word carries the whole philosophy of modern FPS customization.

| Term | What it means |
|------|-------------|
| **Kit** | Your full gear setup before the match |
| **Class** | A preset role or archetype |
| **Build** | Stat-focused optimization, more common in RPGs |

I copy the killcam setup every time. No shame.

## FAQ

**Is this the same as class?**

Not really. Class is the role—Assault, Medic, etc. This is the specific gear you put on that role. One class can have a hundred different kits.

**Can I change it mid-game?**

Depends on the game. Warzone lets you buy a Drop. Apex makes you loot. Most arena shooters lock it. Check your game before you ask in chat.

**How do I explain this to a non-gamer?**

It's like picking your gear before a camping trip. Do you bring the tent, the grill, or just a bag of chips and hope for the best? Same energy.

**Why do players copy each other's setups?**

Because nobody wants to admit they got outplayed. It's easier to say "his gun is better" than "he's better." I've done it. You've done it. We've all done it.
